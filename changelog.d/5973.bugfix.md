`IDAKLUSolver` sensitivities now account for event times that move with the inputs. Sensitivities at each solution time stay partial derivatives at that fixed time.
- `Solution.t_event_sensitivities` gives how the time of the event that ended a solve moves with the inputs. `last_state`, and a step's `first_state`, hold total derivatives along the moving time, so `solution.last_state["Discharge capacity [A.h]"].sensitivities` is the sensitivity of the capacity at a voltage cut-off.
- An event-time sensitivity that is undefined, as at an event tangent to the trajectory, is NaN, and the last state and a step after it treat the event time as fixed for that input.
- An event on time derivatives, such as `x.diff(pybamm.t)`, moves with the derivatives it reads. One already violated when the solver restarts at a breakpoint ends the solve at that breakpoint, which moves with a step's start unless it is a discontinuity at a constant time.
- A step that starts after an event, such as the step after an experiment "until" step, starts from sensitivities at fixed time, so they match finite differences. Before, they ignored the moving start time. `step(end_time_fixed=True)` keeps a step's end time fixed, as experiments do for padding rests and for steps cut short by an end or stop time.
- Time integrals include their moving end points.
- A step whose equations read its start time, such as a drive cycle, only accounts for its moving initial state, and warns, when it has `output_variables` or a discontinuity whose time depends on an input splits it.
